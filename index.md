## About Me
I'm a Staff Research Scientist at [Cohere Labs](https://cohere.com/research), where I conduct research on large language models, centered around multilinguality, reinforcement learning, and evaluation. I am also an associate industry member at [MILA](https://mila.quebec/). I am based in Canada, in the suburbs of Montreal. Previously I worked at [Google Translate (Research), Montreal](https://research.google/locations/montreal/), with a focus on [machine translation](https://research.google/research-areas/machine-translation/). Very broadly speaking, I am interested in the **intersection of natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning**, where multiple languages come into play. I obtained my PhD from Heidelberg University, Germany, under supervision of [Prof. Stefan Riezler](https://www.cl.uni-heidelberg.de/statnlpgroup/members/riezler/) at the [StatNLP group](https://www.cl.uni-heidelberg.de/statnlpgroup/).

## My Research

### RLHF before it was cool
In my PhD, I investigated how *reinforcement learning* algorithms can be used to turn weak supervision signals from users into meaningful updates for a machine translation system. If you're into RLHF, check out my early works on [learning from simulated human feedback](https://aclanthology.org/P17-1138/) (ACL 2016) - we called it "bandit structured prediction" back then, and [learning from actual user feedback](https://aclanthology.org/N18-3012/) (NAACL 2018) for sequence-to-sequence models. You can find many elements (and challenges) of today's leading RL algorithms there, just at smaller scale and before LLMs and the RLHF branding. A few years later we went [back to these basics](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.662/) by taking apart PPO and bringing back good old REINFORCE. I gave a lecture at [AthNLP 2026](https://athnlp.github.io/2026/presentations.html) to summarize these 10 years of RLHF (slides available on the AthNLP page). 

### Accessibility & Inclusion
🎯 My long-term goal for NLP research is to make it more accessible and inclusive, along multiple dimensions: 
1. **Underresourced NLP**: Foster research for underresourced languages and by underrepresented groups, such that not only English-speaking users can benefit from the progress we're making in NLP. I'm particularly interested in helping grassroots communities, such as [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/), grow and mature.
2. **Novices**: Reduce the entry burdens (in terms of coding and research practices) for novices in the field, especially for new students or researchers from other related areas. I am generally generous with my time when it comes to helping out newcomers find their direction and resources to get started with research. The [Cohere Labs Open Science Community](https://cohere.com/research/open-science) is a fantastic place for that, so you can often find me active there (e.g. in the [2026 summer school, sharing insights from my path to research](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDSbz1NYbSw&list=PLLalUvky4CLK3oT1DKNPagd_lTXooYVlR&index=1).
3. **Women**: NLP and ML research is still a male-dominated field, and it can be challenging to navigate for gender minorities. It takes time to grow a network of support - and as a woman it often requires finding your own unique path because there are not many footsteps to follow. This also applies to balancing work and growing a family: I am mom of a two little kids (soon three), so if you'd like to connect to chat about balancing family and research, reach out, and I am motivated to make research a more supportive place for young families.

Check out our lab's [scholar program](https://cohere.com/research/scholars-program) and [Grant calls](https://cohere.com/research/grants), these are unique opportunities for getting started with LLM research.

### Multilingual and Multicultural Modeling
Most leading LLMs today are multilingual, more or less intentionally. I don't see it as optional to evaluate techniques and models across languages, but rather as an obligation for [responsible research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21344) with a global perspective (most critical for [safety](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.800/)). Besides adding one dimension to every evaluation, it is a good reality check for robustness of developed techniques. While multilinguality can seen overwhelming and daunting, it is the perfect opportunity for collaboration, as we can put our global network of researchers into action, e.g. in [data audits](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.4/), or for open [LLM](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.845/) or [data development](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.620/).
More recently, I have focused on the aspect of culture that is found in the layer below language. Even if LLMs get the surface language right, there are large discrepancies for [how well they are able to understand, relate to and generate suitable content for cultures around the globe](https://cohere.com/blog/why-cultural-awareness-is-essential-for-global-ai).

### Evaluation
The faster model development and releases, the more important become evaluations. They represent our compass and proxies to real-world impact. It is incredibly important that we make sure to constantly evolve them, and maintain rigor and the will to look beyond a single score or ranking. I have written about this in two blogposts ([Elo ratings](https://cohere.com/blog/elo-ratings-beyond-arena-style-evaluations), [fair and comprehensive multilingual LLM evaluation practices](https://cohere.com/blog/towards-fair-and-comprehensive-multilingual-and-multicultural-llm-benchmarking)) in collaboration with Singapore, and in a recent COLM paper, where we [draw the connections between LLM and MT evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11829).
